Challenge

Same skill. Three completely different rooms.

Three organizations, three years, three totally different definitions of "stakeholder": a Fortune 500 electronics company's engineering and support teams, a global consumer brand's cross-functional leadership matrix, and an all-volunteer nonprofit media team with no formal reporting structure at all.

Each one needed the same underlying thing — someone who could earn trust quickly, translate complexity into a clear story, and turn that trust into measurable results — but none of them needed it delivered the same way.

Approach

One playbook. Recalibrated each time.

Regardless of the room, the method stays the same: understand what this specific audience actually needs before proposing anything, translate technical or data-heavy work into language they can act on, keep a communication cadence steady enough that people trust you before there's a problem, and follow through on the small things — because that's what the big trust is actually built from.

The stakeholders change. The relationship-first method doesn't.

What I Did

Three organizations, one through-line.

Samsung Electronics America — Content Strategist

Partnered with engineering and support teams to translate conversational AI performance data into content and flow changes that both human agents and the AI itself could act on — reducing call volume by 25% while improving chatbot response accuracy by 15%. The stakeholders here were internal and technical; the job was making sure a support strategist's recommendations landed as clearly for an engineer as they would for a customer.

LG Electronics — Program Manager, CX Innovation

Owned the stakeholder ecosystem behind a company-wide CX transformation — aligning Engineering, Data Science, and regional marketing teams around a shared post-purchase strategy. That alignment work is what made the results possible: 1.5M personalized communications generated monthly, a 15% increase in customer retention, and a 10-point NPS improvement. None of it holds together without every team trusting the same data and the same plan.

Onnuri English Ministry — Community Media & Operations Lead

Built trust with an all-volunteer team and a community audience, with no formal reporting structure to lean on. Mentored contributors, set a sustainable content cadence, and used engagement data to guide creative direction — doubling the team's output capacity while growing organic reach by 113%. The "stakeholders" here were volunteers and a community; the trust had to be earned, not assumed by title.

Results

The range is the proof point.

A Fortune 500 engineering team, a cross-functional corporate leadership matrix, and an all-volunteer nonprofit community don't have much in common on paper. But each one responded to the same approach: understand the audience first, translate the complexity, show up consistently, and let the results speak.

That's the muscle I bring to any client or stakeholder relationship — the ability to read a room I've never worked in before, figure out what "trust" looks like there, and earn it fast enough to move the work forward.
